## 1. Purpose

This SOP explains how employees and front-line managers use Growth Compass to keep personal development front and centre. It aims to:

- Find out what actually motivates each employee, covering both intrinsic and extrinsic rewards, so development plans match what people want and need.
- Find out which basic needs at work (autonomy, competence, relatedness) are not being met, **and what each gap is related to**, so managers can fix causes, not symptoms.
- Capture **how each person works best, in their own words**, so managers can tap into their talents. This supports both employee satisfaction and productivity.
- Give every employee a measurable plan at the pace they choose, sized to the time they decide to invest, with an honest verdict on each goal.
- Keep **skills growth** at the centre: the plan builds skills and certifications the employee keeps, and the app shows the skills earned so far. Promotions, raises and new roles are outcomes those skills prepare them for, inside the company or anywhere else.
- Help each employee elaborate on their talents and record their accomplishments as STAR stories, so they can see their impact and explain it at the next level or in an interview.
- Keep the employee in control of their own development, including skills and certifications. The manager is a **supporting layer**: they respond to requests and create conditions, but they do not dictate what comes next.

### 1.1 Guiding principle: personal development is personal

- The **employee** owns their development and is expected to invest the most in it: the choices, the effort, and, if they wish, some of their own time.
- The **manager** is a supporting layer: they guide, give honest feedback, open doors (time, tasks, funding, mentors) and remove barriers. They do not decide the employee's direction or pace.
- **Skills are portable.** What people learn and master stays with them, whether their next step is inside the company or at another one. Both are treated as legitimate career moves.
- **The employee sets the pace.** How fast someone grows depends on how much they choose to invest. They choose how far ahead to plan (2 weeks to 2 years, their own date, or no deadline), how much work and own time to give, how often to check in and how often to review with their manager. The plan follows them, and they can change any of it at any time.
- **Every skill you earn is yours, wherever your career takes you.** The plan's goals are skills to build. The app never treats a promotion or a pay rise as something a plan can deliver: the skills are the employee's; the openings and decisions belong to others.
- **A job is a way to earn and a way to learn.** Employees are encouraged to name what they want their job to teach them, and to keep a record of skills and lessons they can use anywhere. How strongly this lands depends on each person's motivation, and the app tailors its message accordingly.

## 2. Scope

**In scope:** individual development planning at each person's pace, check-ins at the rhythm they choose, talents and accomplishment (STAR) stories, 1:1 coaching preparation and team-level development planning.

**Out of scope:** Growth Compass must **not** be used for hiring, pay decisions, performance ratings, promotion decisions or discipline. See section 7.

## 3. Roles and responsibilities

| Role | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| **Programme owner** (HR, L&D or operations lead) | Sets up and shares the app, briefs managers, explains purpose and privacy to employees, and reviews this SOP. |
| **Employee** | Completes the assessment honestly, agrees the plan with their manager, sets their own pace, works the plan, checks in at the rhythm they chose, records talents and STAR stories, and retakes the assessment when they reach the date they set, or whenever they choose. |
| **Front-line manager** | Builds their team view, reviews it weekly, uses the coaching playbook and development moves in 1:1s, responds to status flags, reviews progress at the rhythm each person chose, and suggests accomplishment stories without editing the employee's record. |
| **HR / People team** | Supports escalations, especially fairness and pay-equity concerns, disengagement and well-being. |

## 4. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Motivation quality** | Where the person's reasons for working sit on the self-determination continuum. **Autonomous**: they value or enjoy the work. **Controlled**: they work mainly for rewards or from pressure. **Mixed**: a blend of both. **Disengaged**: they see little reason to put in effort. |
| **Needs (A·C·R)** | **A**utonomy (having a say), **C**ompetence (feeling capable) and **R**elatedness (feeling connected), each scored 1 to 5. A score of 2.5 or below counts as low. When a need is low, the employee picks what it is **related to**. |
| **How you work best** | Self-identified work style: alone, with a partner, in a group, or depending on the task. Also covers team identity, productive conditions, peak time, and talents in the person's own words. |
| **Rewards (intrinsic / extrinsic)** | Ten rewards rated by importance. Intrinsic: mastery, autonomy, purpose, learning, belonging. Extrinsic: pay, promotion, recognition, stability, flexibility. |
| **70-20-10** | How plan actions are organised: practice on the job (70), learning from people (20) and formal learning (10). |
| **Development moves** | Concrete actions generated for the manager to take for each direct report. |
| **Team patterns** | What's common across the team, with suggested team-level moves. |
| **Status flag** | An automatic signal in the team table telling the manager who needs attention first (see 5.5). |
| **Talent** | Something the person **already** does well, repeatedly, that others notice and rely on, **backed by evidence** (who said so, or what results show it). Without evidence it is labelled **Self-reported**. |
| **Goal** | A skill the person **can't do yet**, or not well enough, and has to work for. It has a measure ("Success looks like") and, unless they chose no deadline, a due date within their plan. The person marks each goal **Focus now**, **Later** or **Someday**. |
| **Pace** | Everything the employee decides about timing: how far ahead to plan (2 weeks, 1 month, 6 weeks, 3, 6 or 9 months, 1 year, 18 months, 2 years, their own date, or no deadline), how much work time and own time to invest (a quick pick or their own number of hours a week or a month), how often to check in, and how often to review with their manager. **You set the pace. The plan follows you.** It can be changed at any time with **Change my pace**. |
| **Goal verdict** | An honest label on every goal: **Realistic**, **Ambitious** (more than the time you set: consider fewer goals in Focus now), **Unlikely at this pace**, **Not fully in your control** (pay, promotion, recognition or flexibility: the skills are yours, the decision belongs to others), **Later**, **Someday** or, with no deadline, **Step by step** (an honest note on how long things usually take). |
| **Reality check** | Compares the open actions in **Focus now** with the time the employee chose to invest over their horizon (about 2 hours per action). The app suggests moving goals that don't fit to **Later**; the employee can override it, with an honest note. |
| **Skills passport** (skills and certifications tracker) | The employee's own list of skills and certifications. Suggestions are tailored to technical or non-technical roles, and the employee moves each item through its stages. Skills: Planned → Learning → Practising → **Mastered** (3 independent practices with good feedback). Certifications: Planned → Studying → Exam booked → **Certified**. Mastery needs evidence. It belongs to the employee and sits at the centre of **My growth**, with a **Skills earned so far** summary. **Copy my career portfolio** exports it for a CV, LinkedIn or interview prep. |
| **Talent (elaborated)** | For each talent: what I do well, the evidence (who noticed, what results show it), where I use it at work, and how it could add value next (a new level here, or another company). Talents with evidence show **Confirmed**; without evidence, **Self-reported**. |
| **STAR story** | An accomplishment written as **S**ituation, **T**ask, **A**ction, **R**esult, with the result quantified (time, money, quality, customers, people), the skills used, the talent shown, the impact on the organization and the value the person could add next, plus reflection prompts. The STAR method is a behavioral interviewing technique credited to DDI (Development Dimensions International); it is used here as a general way to structure stories and is not the app's invention. |
| **Interview prep** | A view that lists the person's STAR stories grouped by skill. |
| **Support request** | A request from the employee to their manager for time, a real task to practise on, feedback, funding, a mentor, or sign-off of mastery. The manager replies: *I'll arrange it*, *Let's discuss in our 1:1*, *Not possible right now* or *Done*. |

## 5. Procedure

### 5.1 Setup (programme owner, once)

1. Open the app and read the **Blueprint** (this procedure), then the **How to use** and **Foundations** tabs.
2. Share the app with all participating managers and employees with **Contributor** access. People with view-only access can read but cannot save.
3. Send a short announcement to employees that covers:
   - The purpose: their development, in their words.
   - That results are not used for pay, ratings or discipline.
   - Who can see what (see section 7).
   - That it takes about 15 minutes.
4. Brief managers on this SOP. Suggest they turn on **Sample data** and explore the sample team before their own team starts.

### 5.2 Employee procedure

| Step | Action | When |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Open **My growth** → **Take the assessment yourself**. Complete all 10 steps (about 15 minutes). | Day 0 |
| 2 | Read your profile: why you work, needs met and what's behind any gaps, how you work best, rewards, beliefs. | Day 0 |
| 3 | Review your generated plan. Each goal is a skill to build, with a **Success looks like** measure, actions tagged 70 / 20 / 10, and what to **ask of your manager**. Mark each goal **Focus now**, **Later** or **Someday**. | Day 0 |
| 3a | In **Who can see my development**, name your direct manager and, if you want, share chosen parts (profile, plan, skills passport, talents and accomplishments, check-ins) with anyone else, such as a mentor, another manager or HR. Everyone else sees nothing. | Day 0, change any time |
| 4 | Meet your manager to agree the plan. Edit, add or remove actions so it fits. | By week 2 |
| 5 | Work the plan and tick actions off as you complete them. Bring goals from **Later** or **Someday** back into focus whenever you choose. Change your pace at any time with **Change my pace**. | Ongoing |
| 5a | In your **Skills passport**, choose what to work towards next, move each item through its stages, log practice, and add evidence when it's mastered or certified. | Ongoing |
| 5b | When you need help, select **Ask for support** on that item. Say exactly what you need: time, a task, feedback, funding, a mentor or sign-off. | When needed |
| 5c | In **My talents**, elaborate on each talent: what you do well, evidence, where you use it, and the value it could add next. | Any time |
| 5d | In **My accomplishments**, add STAR stories while they're fresh. Quantify the result, link the skills and talent, and answer the reflection prompts. Use **Interview prep** before reviews or interviews, and **Copy my career portfolio** for a CV or LinkedIn. | Any time |
| 6 | Add a **Check-in**: energy, productivity, wins, a lesson you could use in any job, where you used something new on the job, what made the difference, what you need. Turn a win into a STAR story with **Add this win as a STAR story**. | At the rhythm you chose |
| 7 | **Retake** the assessment. You get a new plan, and your previous results are kept in your history. | When you reach your date, or whenever you choose |

The 10 assessment steps:

1. **About you**: technical or non-technical role, time in current role, job title.
2. **Why you work**: nine reasons for putting in effort, rated by how true they are.
3. **Your needs**: autonomy, competence and relatedness. If any is low, you choose what it is related to.
4. **How you work best**: alone or in a group, team identity, productive conditions, peak time and your talents, in your own words.
5. **Rewards**: rate 10 intrinsic and extrinsic rewards.
6. **Top 3**: confirm your top three drivers.
7. **Deep dive**: one follow-up question per top driver, tailored to technical or non-technical roles.
8. **Beliefs**: mindset, goal focus, confidence to learn, whether effort leads to results and results to rewards, and fairness.
9. **Direction**: get ready to lead (here or elsewhere), become the go-to expert, explore another area, grow in your current role, **build a career that can go anywhere** (here or at another company), or "not sure yet". Also: what you want this job to teach you, besides pay. Your manager can see your direction.
10. **Practical: set your pace**: what gets in the way, how far ahead to plan (or no deadline), how much work time and own time you'll invest (quick picks or your own number of hours a week or a month), how often to check in and how often to review with your manager. Your plan is sized to what you choose.

### 5.2.1 Talents vs goals: setting honest expectations

Growth Compass deliberately keeps talents and goals apart, and it does not sugar-coat.

- **A talent is not a goal.** "I'd like to be good at leading people" is a goal. "The team comes to me when a customer escalates, and my lead routes those calls to me" is a talent with evidence. If you can't say who would agree or what results show it, it's a goal, and the app labels it **Self-reported**.
- **Enjoying something is not proof of skill.** What makes you lose track of time shows where to grow. It doesn't show you're already good at it.
- **Your time decides scope.** At 1 to 2 hours a month you have time for about 2 actions in 3 months, and about 1 in 6 weeks. The app suggests moving goals that won't fit to Later instead of pretending they will. You can keep them in focus anyway; it will tell you honestly that they'll take longer.
- **Some outcomes are not in your control.** Pay depends on the pay process and budget. Promotion depends on an opening and on other people's decisions. Recognition depends on how others respond. Flexible arrangements depend on business needs. Your plan builds the skills that make you ready, inside your company or anywhere else. It does not guarantee the outcome.
- **Goals are skills, not titles.** The plan never sets "get promoted" as a goal. It sets the skills a role needs, such as the skills a team leader needs, and records them as they are earned. With no deadline, the app gives honest notes instead, for example that moving into leadership usually takes 12 to 24 months.
- **Mastery takes repetition.** A course starts a skill. Mastery means doing it independently, repeatedly, with good feedback.

### 5.3 Manager procedure

| Step | Action | When |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Share the app with your team. Direct reports appear in **My team** automatically once they name you as their direct manager. Other people appear only if they choose to share with you, marked **Shared with you**, and you see only the parts they shared. | Once |
| 2 | Review the **team table** and **Team patterns**. Check the status column and A·C·R dots first. | Weekly (10 minutes) |
| 3 | Respond to any status flags using the table in 5.5. | Within the stated time |
| 4 | Before each 1:1, select the person and read their **Development moves** and **Coaching playbook**. | Before every 1:1 |
| 5 | In the 1:1, use the suggested questions. Agree one or two development moves and tick them off when done. Record agreements in **Private coaching notes**. | Every 1:1 |
| 5a | Answer every **support request** in the person's panel. Arrange it, discuss it in the 1:1, or say *not possible right now* with a reason and a date. Don't edit or redirect their tracker: they own it. | Within 1 week |
| 5b | Use the **Talents vs goals** card, **My talents** and the goal verdicts. Confirm or challenge self-reported talents honestly. Never promise pay or promotion you don't control. | Every 1:1 |
| 6 | Pick one **team-level move** from Team patterns and run it, for example a huddle on autonomy, a team learning hour or a buddy pairing. | Monthly |
| 5c | If the person shares **Talents and accomplishments** with you, read their STAR stories. When you notice something they did well, use **Suggest a story to capture**. They decide whether to add it; you can't edit their record. | When you notice it |
| 7 | Review the **Is it working?** panel with each person (reaction, learning, behaviour, results), then ask whether they want to retake, keep going or change their pace. | At the date they chose |

**How to address development for each direct report.** The app gives managers three levels of support:

1. **Fix root causes:** for any low need, the playbook gives the specific cause the employee named and the matching fix. For example, low autonomy related to micromanagement → agree outcomes and check-in points, then stop checking the how.
2. **Tap into talent:** use what colleagues come to them for and the work that makes them lose track of time. Build it into their assignments and let them teach it to others.
3. **Set up their work the way they're productive:** use their preferred work mode (alone, partner, group), their best conditions and their peak time when scheduling important work.

**Support, don't dictate.** The employee decides what to develop next and how fast. The manager's job is to remove barriers (time, tasks, feedback, funding, mentors), give honest feedback and confirm mastery when it's real. If you think a choice is unrealistic, say so plainly and explain why. The decision stays with them.

On top of these, development moves cover stretch assignments, delegation, learning time, act-up opportunities, recognition, career conversations and regular informational feedback.

### 5.4 Rhythm at a glance

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Day 0 | Employee takes the assessment |
| By week 2 | 1:1 to agree the plan |
| Every week | Manager scans the team table (10 minutes) |
| At each person's rhythm | Employee check-in + 1:1 on progress, as often as they chose |
| Every month | Manager runs one team-level move |
| Halfway to their date | Review the Foundation goal, if the plan has one |
| At their date | Review results, then retake or change pace |

### 5.5 Responding to status flags

| Status | What it means | Manager action | Timeframe |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Disengagement risk** | Strong agreement that there's little reason to put in effort | Private conversation. Listen first; check workload, fit and well-being. Involve HR if needed. | Within 1 week |
| **Retention risk** | 3+ years in role and a stalled plan or low energy | Career conversation: scope, next role, what would make them stay. | Within 2 weeks |
| **Low energy at last check-in** | Energy rated 2 or below | Ask what's draining them and what would help. | Within 1 week |
| **Fairness concern** | Rates fairness of rewards 2 or below | Explain how decisions are made. Take pay-equity questions to HR. | Next 1:1 |
| **Plan date reached** | The date the employee chose has arrived | Review results together. Ask whether they want to retake, keep going or change their pace. | Within 2 weeks |
| **Plan stalled** | Under 20% done after about a third of their horizon (at least 14 days) | Remove blockers. Shrink the plan to one action they can finish this week. | Next 1:1 |
| **Support ask waiting** | The employee asked for support and hasn't had a reply | Reply: arrange, discuss or "not yet" with a reason and a date. | Within 1 week |
| **Check-in overdue** | No check-in for 5+ days longer than the rhythm they chose (never, if they chose "only when I choose") | Do the check-in together. | Next 1:1 |
| **Assessment not started** | No results yet | Invite them personally and explain the purpose. | This week |
| **On track** | None of the above | Recognise progress specifically. | Ongoing |

## 6. Records

- Each employee's full record (profile, pace, plan, skills passport, talents, accomplishments and check-ins) is stored privately for that employee.
- **The employee decides who sees it.** Their named direct manager sees the full record. Anyone else sees only the sections the employee shares with them. Everyone else sees nothing. The employee can change this at any time.
- In this version, the parts an employee shares are kept in a shared record that the app shows only to the people they chose. For a company-wide rollout, back this with your HR system's access controls.
- Managers' **private coaching notes** and development-move ticks are visible only to that manager.
- A manager's **story suggestions** are saved with the manager's own shared record and shown only to the employee they're for.
- Each employee's last six assessment results are kept in their history when they retake.

## 7. Privacy and appropriate use

1. **Development only.** Results must never be used for hiring, pay, ratings, promotion or discipline decisions.
2. **Employee control and transparency.** Employees choose who sees their development. Before they start, explain that their direct manager will see the full record once named, and that anything else is shared only by their choice. Never ask an employee to share with anyone beyond their direct manager.
3. **Not a clinical or validated test.** The questions were written for this app, following established research frameworks. They are not reproductions of published scales and have not been psychometrically validated. Treat scores as conversation starters.
4. **Snapshot, not a label.** People change; retake when you reach the date you set, or whenever you choose.
5. **Escalation.** Concerns about well-being, discrimination or pay equity go to HR, following existing company policy.

### 7.1 Where information goes

The **Privacy and security** page in the app (link in every page footer, or `#privacy`) explains this to employees in plain language. In summary:

- **Saving:** in the team app on claude.ai, entries save about a second after each change to the person's private section, tied to their claude.ai account. No one else can open it, including their manager, the app owner, and Claude working for the owner. In the public demo or when signed out, entries are saved only in that browser.
- **Sharing:** only the sections a person chooses are copied to a shared record that the app shows to the people they picked. That record is protected by the app, not locked per person in the database (see section 6).
- **No AI processing and no tracking:** answers are not sent to Claude or any other AI service; plans and coaching tips come from fixed rules in the app. No ads, analytics or tracking; the page loads only Google Fonts from outside.
- **Exports:** *Copy my career portfolio* (earned skills, certifications, talents and STAR stories) copies text to the person's clipboard only.
- **Deleting:** anyone can permanently delete their own data with **Delete my data** on the Privacy and security page.

## 8. Troubleshooting

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| "View only" banner, can't save | Ask the programme owner for Contributor access. |
| A direct report doesn't appear in **My team** | They must finish the assessment and name you as their direct manager in **Who can see my development**. |
| Team table is empty | No one has named you as their manager or shared with you yet. Turn on **Sample data** to see how the view works. |
| A person shows **Limited view** | They shared only some sections with you. That's their choice. |
| Switch language | Use **EN / ES** in the top bar. The app remembers your choice on that device. |
| Results look wrong | Retake the assessment. Answers should describe how things are now. |
| "The assessment has been updated" notice | Retake to get a profile based on the current version. |
| A goal says **Later** | It doesn't fit the time you chose yet. Set it to **Focus now** when you're ready, give more time, or choose a longer horizon with **Change my pace**. |
| I don't want a deadline | Choose **No deadline: I'll go step by step** in **Change my pace**. The plan shows your next few steps. |
| A talent says **Self-reported** | Add evidence in **My talents**, or ask your manager or a colleague whether they'd agree. |
| "Log 3 independent practices first" when marking a skill mastered | Use **+1 practice** each time you do it without help, or ask your manager for sign-off. |

## 9. Research foundations

The app's logic draws on the frameworks in these five books (see the **Foundations** tab for where each one appears):

- Ryan, R. M., & Deci, E. L. *Self-Determination Theory: Basic Psychological Needs in Motivation, Development, and Wellness.*
- Elliot, A. J., Dweck, C. S., & Yeager, D. S. (Eds.). *Handbook of Competence and Motivation: Theory and Application* (2nd ed.).
- Ormrod, J. E. *Human Learning* (8th ed.).
- Howes, S. S., & Muchinsky, P. M. *Psychology Applied to Work* (13th ed.).
- Cascio, W. F., & Aguinis, H. *Applied Psychology in Talent Management* (9th ed.).

**STAR method** (Situation, Task, Action, Result): a behavioral interviewing technique credited to DDI (Development Dimensions International). Used here as a general way to structure accomplishment stories; not affiliated with or endorsed by DDI. Source: DDI, https://www.ddi.com/solutions/behavioral-interviewing/star-method
